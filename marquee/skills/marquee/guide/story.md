# Story

A launch film has one job: make a stranger feel the problem, then watch the product end it. No voice, so the words
on screen and the motion carry everything.

## The four chapters

| Chapter | Share of the film | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Intro** | ~14 % | The product is on screen from the first frame (a phone, a notification, the app's key screen), then the name. No logo intro. |
| **Problem** | ~25 % | The feeling, in short lines. Then the problem shown as clutter (the ring) or a moment of loss (the calendar). |
| **Use case** | ~47 % | One person, one job, start to finish, in the real product. Half the film is the product working. |
| **Call to action** | ~14 % | A typed line, then the name, one sentence, and where to get it. Then stop. |

Lengths (seconds):

| Film | Intro | Problem | Use case | Call to action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 0–2 | 2–5 | 5–12.5 | 12.5–15 |
| 30 s | 0–4.5 | 4.5–11 | 11–25.5 | 25.5–31 |
| 45 s | 0–6.2 | 6.2–17.5 | 17.5–38.5 | 38.5–45 |
| 60 s | 0–8 | 8–22 | 22–51 | 51–60 |

A 15 s film drops the ring and the second use-case scene: one problem line, one product moment, the logo.

## The eight rules

1. **The product in the first second.** Open on what the product does: the notification it sends, the screen that
   matters. A logo intro makes people scroll away.
2. **Half the film is the product working.** Most demos spend that half on the problem.
3. **The problem is a feeling, not a statistic.** "Every week, something breaks." beats "68 % of receipts are lost".
   The viewer should recognise themselves before they see the product.
4. **One job, start to finish.** Follow one thing (the headphones, the $96 shop) from trigger to done. Jumping
   between features turns the film into a feature list.
5. **One beat of silence before the turn.** The music cuts for a fraction of a second before the answer.
6. **Without a voice, sound does the talking.** A sound on every tap, pop and typed key.
7. **Show where they are.** The chapter bar tells people how much is left, so they stay.
8. **End on three things:** the name, one line, where to get it. Then stop.

## Writing the words

- **3–7 words a screen.** One idea per screen. If a line needs a comma, it may be two screens.
- **Second person, present tense, everyday words.** "You definitely kept the receipt." not "Users often misplace
  documentation."
- **One accent word per line**, in the brand colour: the word that hurts or the word that helps.
- **A question or a half sentence pulls people on.** "…right?" "It’s in there somewhere." "The return window?"
- **The turn is one or two words**: "Simple." "Meet Tandem." "There's a better way."
- **The call to action plays on a verb**: "Buy it. / Keep it." "Split it. / Forget it." "Ship it. / Sleep."
- **Never** "revolutionary", "seamless", "AI-powered", "all-in-one", "game-changer". Say what it does.

## From the input to the beat sheet

Whatever arrives (one line, a website, screenshots), find these six things first. Ask only for what can't be found
or reasonably invented, and say what you invented.

| Find | Example (Kept) | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Name and one-line promise | Kept: receipts, returns and warranties, handled | Intro, end |
| The person and the moment it hurts | someone whose headphones broke after the return window closed | Problem |
| The one thing to follow | a $349 pair of headphones | Every use-case scene |
| The trigger the product sends | "Return window closes in 2 days" | First frame |
| Two or three steps of the job | scan → details fill in → reminder → one-tap return | Use case |
| Where to get it | kept.app · free on iPhone | End |

From a website: the hero line is usually the promise; the "how it works" section is usually the steps; the pricing
page says where to get it. From screenshots: use them inside the devices (real UI beats drawn UI every time).

Then write the beat sheet before any code, one row per scene:

| t | Chapter | Scene | Words on screen | Blocks | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3.6 | Intro | phone rises, notification drops | (notification text) | mesh, phone, lock, notif | chime 1.6 |
| 3.4–6.4 | Intro | name drops in | Kept · Receipts, returns and warranties. Handled. | letters, mark | hit 3.5, thuds |
| 6.2–9.7 | Problem | typed line, icons hop | Every week, something breaks. | paper, type, hop | type keys |
| … | | | | | |

Show the beat sheet to the person in a few lines if they asked for input; otherwise build it.
